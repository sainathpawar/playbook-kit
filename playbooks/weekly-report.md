## Ask first
- audience: Who will read it? [Leadership | My team | Clients] (skip if: leadership, manager, boss, team, client, customer)
- period: Which week or dates does it cover? (skip if: this week, last week, week of)
- source: Where are the updates (pasted notes, tickets, a file)? (skip if: attached, below, pasted, .md, .docx, .txt)
- format: Where will it be sent? [Email | Word doc | Chat message] (optional)

## Skills
- docx: produce a Word document when that format is asked for (optional)

## Steps
1. Read every source and list what was done, what is in progress and what is blocked.
2. Pick the three most important points for this audience; everything else becomes supporting detail.
3. Write the report using the structure in Output.
4. Write risks and blockers plainly, each with an owner and what help is needed.

## Output
- **Summary**: three bullets, most important first.
- **Done this week**, **Next week**, **Risks and blockers** sections.
- Under one page. Plain language; no internal jargon for leadership or clients.

## Done when
- The first three bullets alone tell the reader how the week went.
- Every risk has an owner and a next step.
- No figure appears that isn't in the sources.

## Examples
- write this week's status update for leadership
- turn these notes into my weekly report
- weekly progress update for the client

## Not for
- a quarterly business review deck
- summarising a single meeting
